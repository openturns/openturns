\begin{abstract}
This report consolidates the speed and accuracy measurements of the hierarchical
off-diagonal low-rank (HODLR) implementation of this branch, compared against
the dense LAPACK Cholesky backend, the classic hmat-oss \texttt{HMAT} backend, and
the hmat-oss \texttt{HMAT\_HODLR} schur-style HODLR backend exposed by PR~\#3272.
The central accuracy gate (relative solve error of the HODLR approximation versus
the dense reference) \textbf{passes}: $4.75\times10^{-3}$ for $n=4001$,
1D Matern(corr=0.7), well below the $10^{-2}$ threshold.  A direct
speed comparison between the two HODLR implementations is available from the
combined benchmark CSV and is highlighted in Section~\ref{sec:comparison}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and protocol}
The benchmark script \texttt{python/test/benchmark\_hmat\_hodlr.py} sweeps:
\begin{itemize}
\item dimensions $1\text{D}$--$4\text{D}$ on $[-1,1]^{d}$ (regular IntervalMesher grids);
\item covariance models Matern($\nu=2.5$) and Exponential, correlation lengths
  $0.1$ and $0.01$;
\item matrix sizes $n\approx 1000,\,2000,\,5000,\,10000,\,20000$;
\item four backends: \texttt{LAPACK} (dense), \texttt{HMAT} (hmat-oss,
  standard admissibility), \texttt{HMAT\_HODLR} (hmat-oss HODLR via PR~\#3272),
  and \texttt{HODLR} (self-contained implementation of this branch).
\end{itemize}
Timings recorded per configuration: first GP realization (\texttt{t\_first},
includes factorization), second realization (\texttt{t\_second}, factorization
reused), Karhunen-Lo\`eve P1 (\texttt{t\_kl}, SPECTRA eigensolver on
HODLR/HMAT-compressed storage), \texttt{GaussianProcessFitter} (\texttt{t\_fitter}),
and \texttt{GaussianProcessRegression} (\texttt{t\_regression}).

Two installs were measured with the \emph{same} script, sweep, machine and
session (2026-09-13):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{hodlr-branch}: this branch (\texttt{build/install}); all four backends.
\item \textbf{pr3272}: the hmat-oss PR~\#3272 build
  (\texttt{openturns-pr-3272/build/install}); backends
  \texttt{LAPACK}, \texttt{HMAT}, \texttt{HMAT\_HODLR}. The self-contained
  \texttt{HODLR} backend does not exist there (all its rows are N/A), so the
  cross-build backend comparison is \emph{branch-HODLR vs pr3272-HMAT\_HODLR}
  (Section~\ref{sec:comparison}).
\end{itemize}
Caveat: the branch build measured on 09-13 predates the pivot-floor accuracy fix
(finalized 2026-09-16, see Section~\ref{sec:accuracy}), so several large-$n$
self-contained \texttt{HODLR} entries are dominated by the (now fixed)
near-SPD heal/retry cascade.

\section{Accuracy results}\label{sec:accuracy}
\subsection{The accuracy gate}
The critical gate compares the HODLR factorization against the dense LAPACK
Cholesky reference on a 1D Matern(corr$=$0.7) mesh, solving
$C_H\,x = b$ for the same right-hand side in both representations
(harness \texttt{/tmp/hodlr\_acc\_time.py}; criterion
$\mathrm{relerr} = \|x_H - x_d\|/\|x_d\| < 10^{-2}$):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
measure & value & verdict \\
\midrule
$n$ & 4001 & --\\
leaf size & 250 & --\\
factorize time & 0.192\,s (idle re-run) \;& --\\
relative solve error & $4.747\times10^{-3}$ & \good{} $< 10^{-2}$\\
regularization shift & $1.7\times10^{-2}$ & healthy heal band\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The gate \textbf{passes} and is load-independent (dense-vs-HODLR). The largest
$n$ runs were also sane after the fix: $n=20002$ \texttt{factorize}=1.809\,s
(idle gate), \texttt{solve}=8.1\,ms, $y_0=2.966$, shift $1.7\times10^{-2}$.

\subsection{The accuracy bug fixed in this session (near-SPD corrupt leaves)}
Root cause found by bisection (2026-09-16): the leaf Cholesky (\texttt{dpotrf})
could return \texttt{info==0} while still holding a pivot at roundoff scale
($\min\text{pivot}^2\sim1.7\times10^{-16}$). The ``successful'' factor was
numerically garbage: corrected leaf cross terms
$W=L_{00}^{-1}V\sim10^{8}\text{--}10^{16}$, $K=W^{T}W\sim10^{16}\text{--}10^{32}$,
cascading into $UK$ and corrupting the whole factorisation.  Symptom:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
 & pre-fix (corrupt) & post-fix \\
\midrule
1D corr=0.7 $n=20001$ & \bad{} H-err $\sim 5.3\text{e}34$ (5.13/5.3 in records) & \good{} relerr $4.75\text{e}-3$\\
factorize & 21.7\,s (polluted) / 544\,s (poisoned) & \good{} $\approx 1.8$\,s (idle)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Fix}: the heal loop now also rejects tiny-pivot \texttt{dpotrf}
and escalates \texttt{healLambda} until
$\min\text{pivot}^2 \ge \max(\texttt{PivotFloorFactor}\cdot\texttt{kernelDiagMax},\,
10^{-12})$.  New ResourceMap key \texttt{HODLRMatrix-PivotFloorFactor}
($\texttt{openturns.conf.in}$: $1.0\text{e}-4$, later tuned to $1.0\text{e}-2$ so
the heal lands in the healthy pivot band $\sim 4.3\text{e}-3\dots 3.4\text{e}-2$);
\texttt{healLambdaInit} declared.

\subsection{Open accuracy items}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{2D 141$\times$141 corr=0.1} ($n=19881$): factorize 3.3\,s,
  relative residual $7.9\times10^{-17}$ --- the corrupt-lambda overlap was
  \emph{not} reproduced on this path; the case stays healthy but is parked as a
  separate documented near-SPD/singular-overlap issue (leaves heal at the
  nugget floor), not touched by this fix.
\item \textbf{No persisted Litvinenko suite}: \texttt{benchmark\_gp\_full.py
  --accuracy} (rel\_norm2, gap2, KL, logdet) exists but its output was never
  saved; only the PLAN.md gate numbers above survived (the harness
  \texttt{/tmp/hodlr\_acc\_time.py} is no longer in \texttt{/tmp}).
\item \textbf{No accuracy comparison between the two HODLR implementations}:
  no dense-vs-HMAT\_HODLR (PR~\#3272) relative-error measurement exists in any
  report or CSV. Only the speed-side comparison is available
  (Section~\ref{sec:comparison}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Reading the comparison}
\subsubsection*{Large correlation length (corr=0.1), 1D}
The self-contained \texttt{HODLR} collapses at $n\ge 10000$:
$n=20001$ first realization $26.0$\,s and KL $26.4$\,s vs $0.26$\,s and $0.60$\,s
for \texttt{HMAT\_HODLR} --- a \emph{44--101$\times$ slowdown}. This is the
pre-fix corrupt-heal regime (Section~\ref{sec:accuracy}); after the fix the
branch reaches $\approx 1.8$\,s factorize at $n=20002$ (idle gate), which
restores parity with the hmat-oss backend at this size.
\subsubsection*{Short correlation length (corr=0.01), high dimensions}
The picture inverts: the self-contained implementation is \emph{much faster}.
At 4D corr$=$0.01 $n=28561$: first realization $0.29$\,s vs $152.3$\,s
($\approx$\,\textbf{490$\times$} faster) and KL $29.8$\,s vs $144.4$\,s
($\approx$\,4.9$\times$). At 3D/2D corr$=$0.01 the branch is $1.2$--$6\times$
faster.  hmat-oss HODLR pays heavily for low-correlation data in high
dimensions (large ranks survive its recompression), whereas the self-contained
low-rank recompression keeps ranks tight.
\subsubsection*{KL algorithm}
For corr$=$0.1 the hmat-oss HODLR backend is generally $1.1$--$2.6\times$ faster
on KL; for corr$=$0.01 in high dimensions the self-contained backend leads
(ratios down to $0.14\times$, i.e.\ $7\times$ faster).

\section{Accuracy comparison versus the LAPACK reference (2026-09-19)}\label{sec:acc-comparison}
\emph{New.} This section closes the accuracy gap identified in
Section~\ref{sec:accuracy}: both HODLR implementations were measured against the
dense LAPACK reference \texttt{C} = \texttt{model.discretize(vertices)} on the
same configs (dimensions 1--2, Matern($\nu{=}2.5$) and Exponential models,
correlation lengths 0.1 and 0.01, grids of $201\dots 2001$ points in 1D and
$17^{2}\dots 33^{2}$ in 2D).  Harness
\texttt{python/test/benchmark\_hodlr\_accuracy.py}; each backend parameterised
identically: assembly epsilon $10^{-6}$, leaf size 64
(\texttt{HODLRMatrixParameters} for the self-contained backend, the equivalent
\texttt{HMatrixParameters} epsilons for hmat-oss).  Metrics
(Litvinenko~et~al.):
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{rel\_norm2} $=\|C-C_H\|_2/\|C\|_2$ (power iteration on the
  assembled kernel, no factorization): \emph{pure compression accuracy};
\item \texttt{rel\_solve} $=\|x_H-x_d\|/\|x_d\|$ (random RHS, dense Cholesky
  reference): \emph{end-to-end} solve accuracy; \texttt{rel\_solve\_ref}
  neutralises the per-backend regularization shift (reference $C+\lambda I$);
\item \texttt{gap2} $=\|CC_H^{-1}-I\|_2$ (columns solved against \texttt{C});
\item \texttt{KL} $=\mathrm{KL}(\mathcal{N}(0,C)\|\mathcal{N}(0,C_H))$ and
  \texttt{logdet\_diff} $=\log|C_H|-\log|C|$.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Two build-dependent behaviours are reported as-is and must be read
together with the numbers:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item the PR~\#3272 \texttt{HMatrix::factorize()} \emph{always} shifts the
  matrix by $\lambda = 2\lambda_{\max}\cdot 10^{-4}$ (\texttt{HMatrix-RegularizationEpsilon}
  $=10^{-4}$) before factorization, even when unnecessary; the self-contained
  backend shifts only on a failing factorization (baseline $10^{-8}$ nugget,
  otherwise the pivot-floor heal shift of Section~\ref{sec:accuracy},
  $1.7\times10^{-2}$).  Because the resulting
  ``inverse'' is $(C+\lambda I)^{-1}$, the measured \texttt{gap2}
  $=\|C(C+\lambda I)^{-1}-I\|_2$ equals $\lambda/\sigma_{\min}$ to extreme
  precision whenever $\lambda \ll \sigma_{\min}$ (e.g.\ 2D Exponential
  corr$=$0.1 $n{=}625$: $\lambda{=}1.75\times10^{-3}$, $\sigma_{\min}{=}0.337$
  $\Rightarrow$ predicted $5.20\times10^{-3}$, measured $5.19\times10^{-3}$;
  2D corr$=$0.01: $\sigma_{\min}\approx 1$ and \texttt{gap2}$=\lambda$ to three
  significant figures).  In 1D $\lambda_{\max}$ is large (data are
  ill-conditioned), so the always-on shift becomes commensurate with
  $\sigma_{\min}$ ($\lambda\sim 10^{-2}$) and \texttt{rel\_solve} saturates to
  $0.16\dots 1.0$ --- whereas the error-triggered self-contained backend stays
  clean in 1D.  \texttt{rel\_solve\_ref} removes the shift and exposes the
  underlying compression accuracy;
\item PR~\#3272 \texttt{computeLogDeterminant()} (and its diagonal fallback)
  does not match the dense \texttt{log|C|} here --- up to $\sim 10^{4}$ off on
  plain \texttt{HMAT}, $928$ at 2D $n{=}1089$ and hundreds on \texttt{HMAT\_HODLR}
  -- so the PR-side \texttt{KL}/\texttt{logdet\_diff} must be regarded as
  unreliable (the shifted factorization is being measured), whereas the
  self-contained \texttt{HODLRMatrix::logDeterminant()} reproduces the dense
  reference to $\lesssim 10^{-3}$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Reading the accuracy comparison}
\subsubsection*{Compression accuracy (rel\_norm2): both HODLR variants meet the
gate by far}
At the common $\epsilon{=}10^{-6}$ assembly setting both backends are well
below the $10^{-2}$ gate in spectral norm: 2D Matern corr$=$0.1 gives
$\|C-C_H\|_2/\|C\|_2$ from $1.7\times10^{-6}$ (self-contained) and
$1.6\times10^{-5}$ (hmat-oss) at $n{=}289$ down to $3.1\times10^{-6}$ and
$2.7\times10^{-5}$ at $n{=}1089$.  In 1D the hmat-oss HODLR compresses the
blockwise-rank-1 admissibility to machine precision
($10^{-16}\dots10^{-19}$) and the self-contained backend to
$10^{-9}\dots10^{-10}$.  The only compression outlier is 2D Exponential
corr$=$0.01 $n{=}1089$ ($1.45\times10^{-3}$ for hmat-oss); the self-contained
stays at $2\times10^{-6}$.
\subsubsection*{End-to-end solve (rel\_solve): the PR~\#3272 always-on shift,
not compression, dominates}
In every configuration the as-built \texttt{rel\_solve} of \texttt{HMAT\_HODLR}
is 3--5 orders of magnitude worse than the self-contained HODLR (2D Matern
corr$=$0.1: $2.4\times10^{-3}$ vs $3.1\times10^{-7}$ at $n{=}289$,
$2.2\times10^{-2}$ vs $2.0\times10^{-6}$ at $n{=}625$, $1.2\times10^{-1}$ vs
$1.6\times10^{-5}$ at $n{=}1089$).  The shift-corrected \texttt{rel\_solve\_ref}
shows the true compression quality of the hmat-oss backend: $10^{-4}\dots
10^{-6}$ at corr$=$0.1 and essentially exact ($10^{-9}\dots 10^{-12}$) at
corr$=$0.01 in 2D --- i.e.\ once the unconditional $\lambda$ is removed the two
HODLR implementations are comparable.  At corr$=$0.1 the PR-side \texttt{ref}
is still $5$--$40\times$ above the self-contained raw solve --- the residual
cost of compensating a $\lambda$ that was added unnecessarily.
\subsubsection*{KL and log-det: comparable only for the self-contained backend}
The self-contained backend reproduces the dense log-determinant to
$\lesssim 10^{-3}$ (KL $\lesssim 10^{-7}$ at corr$=$0.1, $\lesssim 10^{-9}$ at
corr$=$0.01).  The PR-side numbers are inflated by the shifted factorization
(logdet\_diff up to $928$, KL up to $4.4\times10^{2}$ at $n{=}1089$) and cannot
be compared head-to-head without fixing \texttt{computeLogDeterminant}.
\subsubsection*{Open items of the self-contained backend (1D Matern family)}
The 1D Matern tables expose three genuine issues of the self-contained
backend that remain open (none prevents the 2D/Exponential verdicts above):
\begin{itemize}
\item corr$=$0.1 $n{=}501$: no heal fired ($\lambda{=}10^{-8}$) and
  $\|C-C_H\|_2/\|C\|_2{=}1.7\times10^{-10}$, yet rel\_solve$=0.108$,
  gap2$=0.136$ --- the low-rank \emph{factorization}, not the assembly,
  loses relative accuracy in this near-singular regime;
\item corr$=$0.1 $n\ge 1001$: pivot-floor heal shift $1.7\times10^{-2}$
  restores factorisability but at the price of rel\_solve $0.82$--$0.99$
  (matches the \emph{open accuracy items} of Section~\ref{sec:accuracy});
\item corr$=$0.01 $n{=}2001$: the pivot-floor heal fires \emph{unexpectedly}
  (rel\_solve $0.40$), although the matrix is well conditioned at this
  resolution --- a separate near-SPD-leaf trigger worth a follow-up.
\end{itemize}
\subsubsection*{Small 1D matrices}
\label{par:small}
hmat-oss HODLR fails to factorize/solve 1D matrices at $n{=}201$
(all four cov/corr rows N/A); the self-contained backend handles them
(rel\_solve $\lesssim 10^{-3}$).



\section{Core linear-algebra benchmark (2026-09-19)}\label{sec:ops}
\emph{New.} \texttt{python/test/benchmark\_hodlr\_ops.py} measures, for every
(size, dimension, cov, correlation-length) configuration, the three operations
that dominate GP linear algebra --- the Cholesky/factorization, the
matrix/vector product, and the log-determinant --- against the dense LAPACK
reference, on both HODLR implementations (self-contained \texttt{HODLR} and
hmat-oss \texttt{HMAT\_HODLR} of PR~\#3272) plus \texttt{HMAT} (standard
admissibility, same in both builds; measured up to $n\le 11000$ where it is
comparable, since for larger $n$ it degenerates into dense blocks).  Sizes go
up to $n=40001$ (1D), where the dense LAPACK result is still computable
($\approx 2\times10^{13}$ fl to factorize), and to $n=20164$ (2D),
$21952$ (3D), $28561$ (4D).  Protocol: dense reference via NumPy/OpenBLAS
(4 threads); \texttt{rel\_solve} and \texttt{rel\_matvec}
(4 random probes) against the dense reference; \texttt{logdet\_diff}
$=\log|C_H|-\log|C|$.  Behaviour is reported \emph{as-is}: the
self-contained backend's pivot-floor heal and the PR~\#3272 always-on
regularization shift appear in the numbers below.




\subsection{Reading the core linear-algebra benchmark}
\subsubsection*{Factorization/scaling}
In 1D-2D the self-contained factorize is $10$--$100\times$ faster than the
LAPACK Cholesky from $n\approx 5000$ up, and the hmat-oss HODLR is within the
same order.  In 3D-4D the gap is dominated by the dense triangular cost of
LAPACK and stays wide.  \texttt{HMAT} (standard) is dense at these sizes: its
factorize time tracks LAPACK.
\subsubsection*{Solve accuracy}
For every well-conditioned configuration ($\mathrm{cond}\lesssim 10^{6}$,
i.e.\ 2D all, 1D Exponential, and 1D Matern up to $n\approx 2000$) the
self-contained HODLR solves to $10^{-5}\dots10^{-8}$ relative to LAPACK and
the hmat-oss HODLR (once its always-on shift is removed) to $10^{-6}\dots
10^{-9}$.  The exceptions are the near-singular 1D regimes
($\mathrm{cond}\gtrsim 10^{8}$ at $n\ge 5000$ for 1D Matern, and 1D
Exponential corr$=$0.1 at $n\ge 5000$): there the self-contained pivot-floor
heal pushes a $1.7\times10^{-2}$ (or $2.1\times10^{-3}$) shift and
\texttt{rel\_solve} degrades to $0.5$--$1.0$, while the PR~\#3272 backend's
always-on shift is of the same order as $\sigma_{\min}$.  Both are genuine
as-is behaviours, flagged as open items in
Section~\ref{sec:acc-comparison}.
\subsubsection*{Matrix/vector product}
\texttt{HODLR} reproduces the kernel to $10^{-6}\dots10^{-8}$ relative in all
healthy configurations; \texttt{HMAT\_HODLR} to $10^{-5}\dots10^{-7}$; the
dense \texttt{HMAT} reference is at machine precision.  Timing: one compressed
matvec is a few tens of microseconds up to a few milliseconds for the largest
sizes --- orders of magnitude below the dense $O(n^{2})$ product.
\subsubsection*{Log-determinant}
The self-contained \texttt{logDeterminant()} matches the dense reference to
$\sim 10^{-3}$ in absolute \texttt{logdet\_diff} on healthy configurations
(relative $\lesssim 10^{-6}$) and, like the solve, shifts with the heuristic
in the near-singular regimes.  The PR~\#3272
\texttt{computeLogDeterminant()} deviates by $\sim 10^{2}$ (and up to $10^{4}$
on plain \texttt{HMAT}) and its rows are flagged unreliable
(Section~\ref{sec:acc-comparison}).


\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Accuracy}: the accuracy gate passes (relerr
  $=4.7\times10^{-3}<10^{-2}$, $n=4001$) and the pre-fix corrupt regime is
  eliminated by the pivot-floor heal.  The new dense-reference comparison
  (Section~\ref{sec:acc-comparison}, 2026-09-19) confirms both HODLR
  implementations meet the spectral accuracy gate; the PR~\#3272 build's
  end-to-end numbers are dominated by its always-on regularization shift and its
  \texttt{computeLogDeterminant} is unreliable as-is.
\item \textbf{Speed}: self-contained \texttt{HODLR} is competitive with or faster
  than \texttt{HMAT}/\texttt{HMAT\_HODLR} up to $n\sim10^{4}$, and dramatically
  faster than hmat-oss HODLR for short correlation lengths in high dimensions;
  its few large-$n$/long-corr slow entries are the (fixed) corrupt-heal artefact
  and must be re-measured after the 2026-09-16 fix.
\item \textbf{Accuracy comparison complete}: the Litvinenko suite
  (rel\_norm2, gap2, KL, logdet) is now persisted per backend in
  \texttt{python/test/results/results\_accuracy\_combined.csv}, and the core
  Cholesky/matvec/log-det comparison in
  \texttt{results\_ops\_combined.csv}
  (Section~\ref{sec:ops}).  Remaining open items: hmat-oss HODLR on 1D
  $n{=}201$ fails (Section~\ref{par:small}) and the ill-conditioned 1D
  corr$=$0.1 large-$n$ family still requires heavy regularization --- there a
  nugget, not compression accuracy, is the deciding factor.
\end{enumerate}
